Flock product requirements#

Snapshot: 15 October 2026; integrated P2 source candidate at b431cfe. P0/P1 acceptance and deployment evidence remain in validation. Audience: implementers, reviewers and players checking intended behavior. Public campaign levels are 1–100; source indexes are 0–99.

Product and approved engine#

Flock: Little Robot, Big Pastures is a cheerful, true-3D robotic herding game for portrait phones, playable with one thumb. The player steers Pip, a robotic sheepdog, behind a flock to guide it along countryside trails to a pen, fenced field or barn. Rewards buy skills and companions; companions support the player rather than delivering the flock unattended. Preserve the rounded, colorful, handmade countryside and visible robotic character details. The requested visual direction was bright, organic, clay/stop-motion countryside inspired by Aardman; characters and assets remain original.

The original engine request was ThreeRust. Investigation found no publicly documented reusable runtime SDK suitable for this repository; the user approved Three.js as the fallback. The implemented stack is Three.js/WebGL, JavaScript modules and Vite, with procedural geometry/textures and generated audio. Do not describe this as ThreeRust, three-rs or threers. See README.md and package.json.

The current product is a standalone static development build with local browser saves. Accounts, online multiplayer and cloud saves are outside the implemented baseline. Local development and explicit build:dev distributions include isolated QA tools; normal production builds exclude them.

Implemented baseline to preserve#

Play and state#

  • Drag the meadow or movement control to steer; WASD/arrows support desktop. Tap Woof, double-tap the meadow or press Space to bark. Bark applies a short herding burst; the current controller uses a 650 ms burst and 2,100 ms cooldown. Preserve single-thumb access when adding interactions.
  • Pip's proximity pushes robotic flock animals away; grouping behavior respects barriers. Gameplay bodies (Pip, robotic flock and ground helpers) cannot cross fences, stone walls, closed gates, bridge rails, rivers outside decks, trees or the map perimeter. Ambient wildlife can cross fence lines and briefly roam beyond/re-enter the playfield; water and trees still constrain it. Pip's herding signal may pass low wooden fences/rails, but stone walls, barns, water and trees shield it. Companion attendance, movement and delivery use clear ground paths.
  • Delivery requires Pip near the destination, an unheld animal fully inside the shared rotated rectangular enclosure footprint and clear ground attendance. Delivered animals remain saved and roam gently within the body-contracted goal footprint (F-028). First valid arrival starts a 30–60 second recovery countdown; all delivered ends early, otherwise timeout freezes a partial result. HUD and results show score / possible score. Pause, menus, workshop and QA editing do not advance simulation time or admissions.
  • Pause/menu/workshop suspend gameplay and audio. Workshop and results support replay/continuation. Restart meadow resets the current scenario; confirmed Restart Game clears campaign progress, bolts and purchases and starts level 1. Cancelling preserves progress.
  • Music begins after user interaction and shares a mute control with wildlife/weather ambience. Pending audio activation must not undo pause; delayed music scheduling must stay bounded.

Code: main.js (begin, bark, menu, confirmRestart, complete, frame), simulation.js (stepSimulation), audio.js. Regressions: UI, simulation, audio and herding-journey tests under tests/.

Workshop return follows its originating gameplay state: opening it through a paused gameplay menu and closing it resumes play; an intro visit returns to intro, and results return to results. Campaign, map size and routes#

The recorded user baseline asks for a small sheep-to-pen tutorial, increasing flock size and journey distance, procedural fields/rivers/bridges/roads/gates, and progression points unlocking skills and autonomous helpers. Later direction preserves compact single-path levels 1–5, asks for 1–2 routes at levels 6–15 and 1–3 routes from level 16, expands vertical roaming bounds by 3–4× from level 6, and continues increasing the playable expanse. Drone starts at 10; the more expensive Buddy starts at 20; other skills appear across the first fifteen levels. These are intent constraints.

The table below records current generator rules, not newly proposed balance targets. Exact starting count 5, +3 animals per level and cap 65 are implementation-derived, not additional user-specified numbers. The current late generator chooses two or three routes within the requested one-to-three range; it does not generate single-route late levels. Deterministic default seed is index * 7919 + 42; custom seeds are supported by createLevel. Index is clamped to 0–99.

Examples: level 8 is index 7, has 26 robots and two routes, and is the first level using stone wall segments; level 17 is index 16 and is the first level eligible for conditional straggler bolts. The special wide level-2 crossing repaired a demonstrated simulated retrieval stall; preserve that regression when changing bridge rules.

Maps provide alternate trails, winding roads, real bridge/gate openings, destination staging room, continuous rivers beyond boundaries and decorative hills outside playable ground. Alternate routes are generated by level rules, not purchased or independently unlocked. See world.js (createLevel, canOccupy, terrainHeight, navigationTarget, openingSteer) and world/journey tests. Geometry-route reachability is not proof that every human can deliver every flock.

Progress, economy and helpers#

The initial profile has zero bolts, no upgrades, no completed levels and only level 1 available. Any positive delivery at completion unlocks the next meadow (capped at level 100). Possible score is 80 + 25 * index; earned score is floor(possibleScore * saved / total). A run pays only max(0, score - previousLifetimeBestEarnedBolts). Equal/worse replays pay zero; later improvement pays only the delta even after spending/reloading. For example, a best score of 32 followed by 64 adds 32 bolts. Zero deliveries neither pay nor unlock. Profile version 2 stores an authoritative bestEarnedBolts ledger; legacy completed levels migrate to fully earned entitlements without crediting spendable points again. Purchased tiers remain saved, but only tiers unlocked for the meadow being played apply. Invalid profile fields are sanitized; legacy boolean purchases migrate to tier 1. Browser storage failure retains the session and shows feedback. Key: flock-foundry-v1; do not clear or rename it for F-025. F-029 records this existing, tested rule as Done; the latest P0 missing-bolts-on-replay report was withdrawn as invalid. See F-029 in the issue tracker.

Art, wildlife and weather#

Current sheep/cows have articulated, instanced stop-motion leg poses. Ground uses deterministic tileable color/bump textures, with decorative grass, flowers and reeds. Foliage includes larger foreground trees, textured bark and leaf surfaces, plus seeded clusters of varied, non-colliding bushes placed away from routes, water, trees, fences, spawns and destinations. Squirrels and rabbits have merged anatomical body meshes and animated paw instances; squirrel/rabbit/bird/butterfly populations scale reproducibly with usable meadow area and remain capped. Wildlife can cross fence lines and roam within a small outside margin, while trees, water, barn walls and stone walls continue to block ground fauna. These are P2 source candidates, pending visual and gameplay acceptance.

weather.js creates one seeded mutable state with type, rainIntensity, snowIntensity, windIntensity, severity, cloudCoverage, daylight, lightningId, lightningAge, thunderDelay and lightningFlash; MeadowNature.update advances it once per unpaused presentation step (playing, intro and results modes), and atmosphere, water and audio read the same object. Levels 1–5 stay sunny; levels 6–8 cycle sun/wind/rain/sun; level 9 onward also cycles storm and snow. Episodes last 90 seconds and blend for 18 seconds. Atmosphere uses ten soft-textured cloud clusters, opacity .10–.12 with cover-driven size, tint, shadow and light changes. Storm severity can produce a deterministic .22-second flash every 24 seconds, with a paired delayed synthesized thunder event. Candidate wildlife shelter thresholds are butterfly .54, squirrel .58, bird .64 and rabbit .76 severity, with .12 return hysteresis; default rain severity .35 is below these thresholds. Rendered shelter/return behavior still needs review. Candidate wetness builds at .012 × rainIntensity per second and dries at .004/s in sun (.0012/s otherwise); seeded safe-land puddles fill at .04 × rainIntensity/s and become visible above .28, then evaporate at .006/s in sun (.0008/s otherwise). Moving actors create bounded splashes only while crossing an accumulated puddle. None of these choices change movement speed or rewards. Focused source tests do not establish visual quality or phone performance.

Decisions still open#

Resolve only the choices needed for an implementation; independent repairs can proceed. Record actual approvals in ISSUES.md. The following are not approved defaults:

  • F-006/F-007: gate closing is not implemented; wall appearance/height and herding-signal behavior need acceptance.
  • F-008/F-023: whether “10–25%” means opacity 0.10–0.25, how overlapping blobs compose, and any storm exception.
  • F-011: whether Buddy shares Pip's tail convention on a real Buddy bark event.
  • F-012: source currently chooses floor rounding, a seeded five-second opportunity cadence, 4–7 units of wander distance, and bolts as a conditional roll; test outcomes and human pacing remain open. A 33% chance is not permission to roll every frame. Small flocks can have a zero-sized eligible cohort under floor rounding.
  • F-017/F-019/F-020: wildlife area/count ranges, caps, absence/spawn rules; bush collision policy; tree scale/range.
  • F-022–F-024: candidate values are now in source (see above), but species thresholds, transition timing, lightning frequency/intensity, wetness/drying rates, puddle placement, river response and actor splash rules still need acceptance. No weather movement penalty is approved.

This is the current source candidate's ownership boundary: MeadowNature advances one shared weather state; atmosphere, water and audio consume it. Candidate values remain subject to P2 review and acceptance.
